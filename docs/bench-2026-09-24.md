# Bench runbook: the 2026-09-24 sitting

One sitting in priority order: P0 BLE reliability first, then the screamers transport steps, then one real match.
The outdoor range ladder and the house sweep run on their own days. Each step gives the command, the pass or fail
rule, and what to log. The method is the [`bench-session` skill](../.claude/skills/bench-session/SKILL.md): the
agent drives the tools, Tony handles the hardware and types `1` when a step is done, and every result has a control,
run first. Stop a block when time runs out; the order is the priority.

| block | what | rows | time | kit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | preflight | - | 15 min | everything |
| 1 | connect reliability | F297, F293 | 45 min | 1 gun and headset, laptop, both Pixels |
| 2 | screamers transport | F269, F270, F272 | 45 min | 1 gun and headset, laptop |
| 3 | trigger order and fire interval | F308, F291 | 15 min | 1 gun, laptop |
| 4 | one real match | S56, F308, F291, F292, F298, F296, F309, F311, F310 | 60 min | 2 guns, both Pixels, MC |
| 5 | outdoor range ladder | F275 | 60 min | 2 guns, outdoor, the rig |
| 6 | super indoor, in Tony's house | S48, F198 | 60 min | 2 guns and headsets, laptop |
| 7 | the IR callout bus | S57 | 40 min | 2 guns, laptop, the ESP32 receiver |

Blocks 0 to 4 fit one indoor sitting of about three hours. Block 5 needs outdoor space. Block 6 runs in Tony's
house, not at the bench. Block 7 fits any indoor sitting with time left; its step 7 needs 30 m.

## Conventions

- **The environment file.** The agent's shell keeps no variables between commands, so every command below starts
  with `source ~/bench-0924.env &&`. Block 0 writes the file. `PY` is Windows Python, the only one with Bluetooth;
  `WPY` is WSL Python, for frame generation only.
- Run every command from the main checkout, `/home/tony/gitrepos/battlecompany`. The Windows venv is an editable
  install of that checkout's `mcp/`, so it runs whatever that checkout holds. Arguments go on the command line
  only: environment variables do not cross from WSL to Windows.
- **Single-quote every frame** in a `raw-bytes` argument. In double quotes the shell expands `$AMMO` to nothing.
- One central at a time: `raw-bytes` and `connect-metrics` open their own link, so disconnect the MCP session (and
  keep the phones off that gun) before each one.
- Bench audio is `$VOL,65` (every `armgen.py` line below passes `volume=65`). Never end a block on a bare `$CLEAR`
  (F11). Power-cycle the gun at the end of a block and disconnect the MCP session.
- A **LOCK-UP**, a **LINK DROP** and a **BAD FRAME** are defined in the screamers sheet ("Rules for every session").
  On a lock-up, the agent connects with the MCP tool and sends `$PLAYX,0,*`, then `$HLED,,6,*`, then `$STOP,*` with
  `send`, notes which got through, then Tony power-cycles the gun and headset, and the gun is re-armed.
- **Reading a phone.** `$PY mcp/tools/webview_eval.py "<js>" --serial <phone ip:port>`. One call takes about a
  second, so read AFTER the action, not during it. `window.brx.link.frames` is the last 60 frames in and out, each
  `{t, dir, f}` with `t` in ms; `window.brx.log` lines carry `[HH:MM:SS]`, good to about 1 s.

## Block 1: connect reliability, P0 (45 min)

The question: how long does a cold connect take, how often does the first attempt work, and does the headset drop
in the first 30 s? The laptop run is the control: it has no Android Bluetooth stack in the way.

**1.1 Laptop, 10 cold connects (F297).** Tony runs this in his own WSL terminal, because it waits for Enter:
`source ~/bench-0924.env && $PY -m brx_mcp connect-metrics $GUN --runs 10`
Before each run it asks him to power-cycle the gun and the headset, then press Enter.
- **Log:** the summary (first-attempt success rate, link time median, p90 and max, headset drops, never-linked
  count, errors) and the JSONL path it prints.
- **Pass or fail:** no bar yet; this is the first measurement. Flag any run with a headset or BLE drop inside 30 s.

**1.2 Each Pixel, 5 cold connects (F297).** For each try: power-cycle the gun and the headset, wait for the headset
to settle, then Tony taps the gun in the picker and starts a stopwatch; he stops it at "linked" on the HUD. Then:
`source ~/bench-0924.env && $PY mcp/tools/webview_eval.py "window.brx.log.slice(-40)" --serial <phone>`
- **Log per try:** the stopwatch time; the "connecting to", "connect i/5 failed after N ms" and "gun … linked"
  lines; whether "*** gun disconnected ***" or "gun keeps dropping" appears within 30 s of the link.
- **Pass or fail:** no bar yet. A phone that drops while the laptop in 1.1 did not points F293 at the phone.

**1.3 Only if 1.2 shows the link loop (F293).** One variable at a time, A/B/A:
1. **A:** on the looping phone, turn on Developer options, set "Bluetooth HCI snoop log" to **Enabled** (not
   Filtered, which cuts the payloads short), then toggle Bluetooth off and on. Reproduce the loop for about 60 s, then `adb -s <phone> bugreport bugreport-f293-a1.zip`. Keep the phone
   still for 5 to 10 min while it writes.
2. **B:** turn off Fast Pair and Nearby scanning (Settings, Google, Devices and sharing, Devices, "Scan for nearby
   devices" off). Power-cycle the headset, connect again, watch 60 s, and take `bugreport-f293-b.zip`.
3. **A again:** turn scanning back on and repeat step 2's connect and bugreport (`bugreport-f293-a2.zip`).
- **Log:** loop yes or no in each run, each bugreport file name, and the time of each drop from the phone log.
- **Read each bugreport** with brx5's tool (on `main` once `lane-brx5/f293-phone` lands; until then
  `python -m brx_mcp.btsnoop` gives the ATT traffic only):
  `source ~/bench-0924.env && $PY -m brx_mcp.btlink bugreport-f293-a1.zip --gun $GUN --frames`.
  It ends with one DROP line per drop and a tally. Each is a reading, not a verdict: `gun-host` drops mean the gun
  ended the link (check the last gun frame and the headset LED); `link-loss` means a supervision timeout (check the
  parameter, data-length and MTU lines just before); `phone-host` means our app or Android sent the disconnect; a
  connect to a peer that is not the gun means the phone is touching the headset.
- **Pass or fail:** the question is answered only if B differs from both A runs.

## Block 3: trigger order and fire interval (15 min)

One gun on the MCP session, no phone.

**3.1 F308: does every release reach the host, in order?** The recoil burst reset depends on `$BUT,0,0`.
1. The agent connects and sends `$WPY mcp/tools/armgen.py 1 5 ar volume=65` with `send_batch`.
2. **Control:** one single shot. `get_events` must show one `$BUT,0,1`, one `$ALCD`, one `$BUT,0,0`, in that order.
3. Five bursts, the trigger held about 2 s each, a full release and a reload between bursts. Type `1`.
4. The agent reads `get_events` once, with `max_events=2000`.
- **Log:** the count of `$BUT,0,1` and `$BUT,0,0`, and per burst the seq of its last `$ALCD` against its `$BUT,0,0`.

The procedure is the F275 row: an F231-style shaded ladder against `t13`, with the same rig, the same shading fix
and the discard-the-first-two-pulls control (F232). The shooter carries the compiled Shotgun with `t13` edited

```
source ~/bench-0924.env && cd mcp && $WPY -c "from brx_mcp.mc.compile import WeaponCatalog as C; f = C().resolve('shotgun', 0).split(','); f[14] = '<t13>'; print(','.join(f))"
```

## Block 6: super indoor, in Tony's house (S48, F198; 60 min)

Tony's definition (S48, 2026-09-23): "super indoor" is a suburban house with light eggshell paint, which reflects IR
strongly, so a shot at the ceiling or a wall bounces and still hits. **Acceptance:** at least 95% of aimed shots
across a room hit, and at most 5% of deliberate bounce shots hit. The grid is `$WEAP` t2 on the shooter against
`$GSET` t2 on the victim. brx5 is reading the firmware for more IR controls; any it finds join this grid.

⚠️ **What t2 does.** `$WEAP` t2 sets the emitter's carrier frequency, not its power (S48, F231, levers §20). A low
value detunes the word away from the receiver's band, which costs reception at every distance. The sweep can still
work: a bounced word arrives weaker than an aimed one, so a detune may lose the bounce first. F231's outdoor ladder
put the transition between about 13 and 50 (13 landed 3 of 8 at 15 m, 25 and 32 landed 15 of 32 at 10 m, 50 landed
21 of 23), and 5 landed nothing. So the grid uses 100 (stock, the control), 50, 32, 25 and 19. `$GSET` t2 = 1 made
a receiver "nearly deaf" in the 2026-09-13 field (F198); that is why it is the other axis.

**Setup.** The laptop's MCP session holds both guns. Use each gun's usual alias (a `connect` alias is saved per
address); below, "shooter" and "victim" name them. Mark a firing spot across
the room from the victim (log the distance), and pick a bounce point on the ceiling or a wall well off the victim
(log where). The victim's `$SIR` table is one fn-28 row, so every hit sends `$HIR` and no pool changes: 400 shots
cannot kill it. Every cell is a full re-arm, which also restores the magazine (a `$WEAP` write resets it).

Victim, `$GSET` t2 = `<s>` (0 or 1); the `sed` edits token 2 of the arm's `$GSET` frame:

```
source ~/bench-0924.env && $WPY mcp/tools/armgen.py 2 6 ar volume=65 -sir=all '+sir=$SIR,0,0,,28,0,0,1,,*' 2>/dev/null | sed -E 's/^(\$GSET,[^,]*,)0,/\1<s>,/'
```

Shooter, `$WEAP` t2 = `<p>`:

```
source ~/bench-0924.env && $WPY mcp/tools/armgen.py 1 5 ar volume=65 t2=<p> 2>/dev/null
```

The agent sends each printed sequence with `send_batch` to its alias. Check the victim's `$GSET` line reads
`$GSET,1,<s>,…` and the shooter's `$WEAP,0` line reads `$WEAP,0,,<p>,…` before any shot.

**Per cell:** arm both guns. Tony fires 20 aimed shots at the victim's headset from the mark and types `1`; then 20
shots at the bounce point and types `1`. Tony reloads between the two windows (40 shots
is more than the 32-round magazine). The agent counts the victim's `$HIR` frames in each window from `get_events`
on the victim, and the shooter's `$ALCD` count as shots fired.

| order | shooter t2 | victim `$GSET` t2 | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 100 | 0 | opening control: today's shipped pair; expect the bounce problem |
| 2-5 | 50, 32, 25, 19 | 0 | the detune ladder at normal sensitivity |
| 6 | 100 | 1 | sensitivity alone |
| 7-10 | 50, 32, 25, 19 | 1 | both levers together |
| 11 | 100 | 0 | closing control: must reproduce cell 1, or every cell between is void |

- **Log per cell:** shots fired and hits, aimed and bounce. Also any hit that Tony did not aim anywhere near.
- **Pass:** a cell passes with at least 19 of 20 aimed hits and at most 1 of 20 bounce hits. Re-run a passing cell
  once more before it counts, then log the winning pair for S48's `house` venue.
- **Stop early** if a column's aimed hits fall below 19 of 20: lower values in that column only get worse.

**Restore (always, even after a void run).** Re-arm both guns stock (`armgen.py 1 5 ar volume=65` and
`armgen.py 2 6 ar volume=65`, no edits), fire one aimed shot and confirm it hits. Then power-cycle both guns and
both headsets, so no gun is left detuned or deaf.

## Block 7: the IR callout bus (S57; 55 min)

Steps from brx5 for brx4's design (S57): a phone that knows a game event has its gun broadcast one `$IRTX` word,
and other guns hear it. Two guns on the MCP session; the ESP32 receiver (`native_capture.py`) faces A's headset
domes as the emit control. Already proven on 2026-09-18, so not re-run: a dead gun forwards a host `$IRTX`, and
magnitudes 1-39 are silent with player 0.

**Arm.** Sender A on team 2, receiver B on team 1, both with the `<15,0>` fn-28 row and friendly fire off:

```
source ~/bench-0924.env && $WPY mcp/tools/armgen.py 2 5 ar volume=65 friendly_fire=0 '+sir=$SIR,15,0,,28,0,0,1,,*' 2>/dev/null
source ~/bench-0924.env && $WPY mcp/tools/armgen.py 1 6 ar volume=65 friendly_fire=0 '+sir=$SIR,15,0,,28,0,0,1,,*' 2>/dev/null
```

The word, sent from A with `send`: `$IRTX,100,15,<pid>,<field4>,<mag>,0,0,100,1,,0,*`. Log every `$HIR` on both
guns with its time and first field (the sensor).

1. **Self-report, live sender.** 5 words from live A, magnitude 21, 3 s apart. Log B's `$HIR` count, and whether A
   reports its own word.
2. **Dead sender.** Kill A with `$BUMP,-9999,1,1,1,,*`; `$LIFE,*` must answer `$HP,0,0,0`. Repeat step 1.
   Pass: B gets 5 of 5. Log any self-report on A.
3. **Dead receiver.** Revive A (`$LIFE,30,0,0,1,*`) and kill B. 5 words: expect none, since fn 28 is ignored on a
   dead gun. Then set B's row to fn 34 (`$SIR,15,0,,34,,,,,*`) and send 5 more. Pass: B sends `$HIR` while `$HP`
   stays 0,0,0 and B stays dead; read `$LIFE,*` after each word, and log B's headset LEDs too. If fn 34 registers
   silently and B stays dead, one row change lets dead players hear callouts. Restore fn 28 and revive B.
4. **Team gating.** B live on team 1: 5 words each with field 4 = 0, 1, 2 and 3. Move B to team 2 and repeat. Log
   which field-4 values register on each team. Field 4 = 2 is the key value: if every team hears a team-2 word, S57
   uses a constant 2, so log it against both receiver teams as carefully as 0 and 3. Watch for the team-2 neutral trap.
5. **Duplicates.** 20 single words 3 s apart, with B at 2 m and again at 0.5 m, domes facing. Per word: how many
   `$HIR` arrive, from which sensors, the gap from first to last, and the ESP32's count. Pass for brx4's 600 ms
   de-duplication: every duplicate lands within 600 ms of its first.
6. **Native reaction.** The exact frame with a non-zero player id, magnitudes 21 to 28, one each. Listen for sound,
   watch B's headset LED, and compare `$LIFE,*` before and after.
7. **Range.** With A dead, 10 words at 3, 10, 20 and 30 m, domes toward B. Then 10 live shots from A's barrel on a
   normal weapon (t2 100) at the same marks; drop the first two live shots (F232). One indoor run from the next
