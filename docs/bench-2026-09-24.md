## Conventions

- **The environment file.** The agent's shell keeps no variables between commands, so every command below starts
  with `source ~/bench-0924.env &&`. Block 0 writes the file. `PY` is Windows Python, the only one with Bluetooth;
  `WPY` is WSL Python, for frame generation only.
- Run every command from the main checkout, `/home/tony/gitrepos/battlecompany`. The Windows venv is an editable
  install of that checkout's `mcp/`, so it runs whatever that checkout holds. Arguments go on the command line
  only: environment variables do not cross from WSL to Windows.
- **Single-quote every frame** in a `raw-bytes` argument. In double quotes the shell expands `$AMMO` to nothing.
- One central at a time: `raw-bytes` and `connect-metrics` open their own link, so disconnect the MCP session (and
  keep the phones off that gun) before each one.
- Bench audio is `$VOL,65` (every `armgen.py` line below passes `volume=65`). Never end a block on a bare `$CLEAR`
  (F11). Power-cycle the gun at the end of a block and disconnect the MCP session.
- A **LOCK-UP**, a **LINK DROP** and a **BAD FRAME** are defined in the screamers sheet ("Rules for every session").
  On a lock-up, the agent connects with the MCP tool and sends `$PLAYX,0,*`, then `$HLED,,6,*`, then `$STOP,*` with
  `send`, notes which got through, then Tony power-cycles the gun and headset, and the gun is re-armed.
- **Reading a phone.** `$PY mcp/tools/webview_eval.py "<js>" --serial <phone ip:port> --port 9229`. On this laptop a
  Windows portproxy rule holds the default port 9222, so pass `--port`. One call takes about a
  second, so read AFTER the action, not during it. `window.brx.link.frames` is the last 60 frames in and out, each
  `{t, dir, f}` with `t` in ms; `window.brx.log` lines carry `[HH:MM:SS]`, good to about 1 s.

## Block 1: connect reliability, P0 (45 min)

The question: how long does a cold connect take, how often does the first attempt work, and does the headset drop
in the first 30 s? The laptop run is the control: it has no Android Bluetooth stack in the way.

**1.1 Laptop, 10 cold connects (F297).** Tony runs this in his own WSL terminal, because it waits for Enter:
`source ~/bench-0924.env && $PY -m brx_mcp connect-metrics $GUN --runs 10`
Before each run it asks him to power-cycle the gun and the headset, then press Enter.
- **Log:** the summary (first-attempt success rate, link time median, p90 and max, headset drops, never-linked
  count, errors) and the JSONL path it prints.
- **Pass or fail:** no bar yet; this is the first measurement. Flag any run with a headset or BLE drop inside 30 s.

**1.2 Each Pixel, 5 cold connects (F297).** For each try: power-cycle the gun and the headset, wait for the headset
to settle, then Tony taps the gun in the picker and starts a stopwatch; he stops it at "linked" on the HUD. Then:
`source ~/bench-0924.env && $PY mcp/tools/webview_eval.py "window.brx.log.slice(-40)" --serial <phone>`
- **Log per try:** the stopwatch time; the "connecting to", "connect i/5 failed after N ms" and "gun … linked"
  lines; whether "*** gun disconnected ***" or "gun keeps dropping" appears within 30 s of the link.
- **Pass or fail:** no bar yet. A phone that drops while the laptop in 1.1 did not points F293 at the phone.

**1.3 Only if 1.2 shows the link loop (F293).** One variable at a time, A/B/A:
1. **A:** on the looping phone, turn on Developer options, set "Bluetooth HCI snoop log" to **Enabled** (not
   Filtered, which cuts the payloads short), then toggle Bluetooth off and on. Reproduce the loop for about 60 s, then `adb -s <phone> bugreport bugreport-f293-a1.zip`. Keep the phone
   still for 5 to 10 min while it writes.
2. **B:** turn off Fast Pair and Nearby scanning (Settings, Google, Devices and sharing, Devices, "Scan for nearby
   devices" off). Power-cycle the headset, connect again, watch 60 s, and take `bugreport-f293-b.zip`.
3. **A again:** turn scanning back on and repeat step 2's connect and bugreport (`bugreport-f293-a2.zip`).
- **Log:** loop yes or no in each run, each bugreport file name, and the time of each drop from the phone log.
- **Read each bugreport** with brx5's tool (on `main` once `lane-brx5/f293-phone` lands; until then
  `python -m brx_mcp.btsnoop` gives the ATT traffic only):
  `source ~/bench-0924.env && $PY -m brx_mcp.btlink bugreport-f293-a1.zip --gun $GUN --frames`.
  It ends with one DROP line per drop and a tally. Each is a reading, not a verdict: `gun-host` drops mean the gun
  ended the link (check the last gun frame and the headset LED); `link-loss` means a supervision timeout (check the
  parameter, data-length and MTU lines just before); `phone-host` means our app or Android sent the disconnect; a
  connect to a peer that is not the gun means the phone is touching the headset.
- **Pass or fail:** the question is answered only if B differs from both A runs.

**1.4 Re-check the F293 fix (the `$VERSION` headset probe, on `main` since `ffbc987c`; 30 min).** The phone needs a
build from `main` (`cd app && npm run android:install`). Its log shows a "headset probe" line per connect. A/B/A, then the cap:
1. **A (new build, app open) x3:** power-cycle the gun and headset with the app open. Log gun power-on to the
   headset-connected sound (stopwatch), loop yes or no, gun power-on to "HUD linked with no warnings", and the phone
   log from `webview_eval.py "window.brx.log.slice(-40)" --serial <phone> --port 9229`.
2. **B (app force-stopped) x2:** the same, as the control (2026-09-24 measured 15 and 16 s).
3. **A x1 again.**
4. **Headset left OFF x1:** the HUD reaches "HEADSET NOT JOINED · POWER-CYCLE THE HEADSET" at 60 s, and the phone
   does not loop.
5. **Mid-match x1:** headset power-cycled during a LIVE match; confirm the 15 s retry cadence past the cap does not
   keep the headset from rejoining.
- **Pass or fail:** pass if there is no loop in 4 A runs, the A headset-join time is within 3 s of the B median, and
  the HUD clears within 3 s of the first `hds.N` in the phone log.
- **If unclear:** repeat with `HEADSET_JOIN_MODE = 'hold'` (`app/src/brxlink.js`).

**1.5 A second phone's SCAN AGAIN during a headset join (follow-up from the 2026-09-24 evening flap, F293; 20
min).** A/B/A, one gun and headset, a second phone standing by.
1. **A:** power-cycle the gun and headset; while the first phone connects and the headset is still joining, have

3. **A again:** repeat step 1 once more.

## Block 6: super indoor, in Tony's house (S48, F198; 60 min, plus 15 optional)

Tony's definition (S48, 2026-09-23): "super indoor" is a suburban house with light eggshell paint, which reflects IR
strongly, so a shot at the ceiling or a wall bounces and still hits. **Acceptance:** at least 95% of aimed shots
across a room hit, and at most 5% of deliberate bounce shots hit. The grid is `$WEAP` t2 on the shooter against
`$GSET` t2 on the victim. brx5 is reading the firmware for more IR controls; any it finds join this grid.

⚠️ **What t2 does.** `$WEAP` t2 sets the emitter's carrier frequency, not its power (S48, F231, levers §20). A low
value detunes the word away from the receiver's band, which costs reception at every distance. The sweep can still
work: a bounced word arrives weaker than an aimed one, so a detune may lose the bounce first. F231's outdoor ladder
put the transition between about 13 and 50 (13 landed 3 of 8 at 15 m, 25 and 32 landed 15 of 32 at 10 m, 50 landed
21 of 23), and 5 landed nothing. So the grid uses 100 (stock, the control), 50, 32, 25 and 19. `$GSET` t2 = 1 made
a receiver "nearly deaf" in the 2026-09-13 field (F198); that is why it is the other axis.

**6.0 Optional, needs a scope: carrier and duty (S48; 15 min).** Reading (v4.32 and v2.08b emitters): the carrier
is `38000 - 125 × (100 - t2)` Hz below t2 = 100, and the gun's indoor or outdoor mode picks one of two fixed output
settings (the S48 row gives about 20% against 38% duty). If that holds, the only emitted-power lever is the
physical mode. Put an IR photodiode on the scope probe, 5 cm in front of the shooter's emitter. Do not use a
VS1838B: it demodulates the carrier away. Arm the shooter stock (`armgen.py 1 5 ar volume=65`, t2 = 100).
1. **A:** the gun powered on in outdoor mode (ALT at power-on, [`manual/hardware.md`](manual/hardware.md)). Three
   single shots. Log the carrier frequency and the duty.
2. **B:** power-cycle into indoor mode, re-arm, three shots.
3. **A again:** outdoor mode, three shots. It must reproduce step 1.
4. In outdoor mode, re-arm with `$GSET` t2 = 1 (the victim `sed` below, on the shooter's arm), three shots.
- **Pass or fail:** a duty change between A and B CONFIRMS a physical power lever. A duty change in step 4 means
  `$GSET` t2 reaches it over BLE, which S48 could build on. Send no `$IRLVL` or `$INDOOR`: neither is on the
  known-safe list. Restore: power-cycle the shooter, then arm it for the grid.
