### 2026-11-02 (desk) · brx4 · the mc-recap-next lander flake (F664)

- The lander logged `mc-recap-next` failing twice in 7 days (2026-11-01 and 2026-11-02), both on step `real/m23`: the
  header read `0:08 OF 10:00` while the test computed `0:07` from `/api/state`.
- Cause: `Session.snapshot()` stamped `t` from one clock read, and `settling()` read the clock again for
  `since_end_ms`. The console and the test both compute the length as `round((t - since_end_ms - go_live_t) / 1000)`,
  so each snapshot carried a few ms of its own drift. A length near a half second rounded differently on the page's
  snapshot and on the test's.
- Fix: `snapshot()` passes its `now` into `recap()` and `settling()`, so `t - since_end_ms` is exactly `end_t`. The
  console's mock backend had the same double read and got the same fix. Test:
  `test_one_snapshot_reads_one_clock_so_the_recap_length_never_drifts` (a clock that moves 7 ms a read). Without the
  fix it fails; with it, it passes.

### 2026-11-09 (desk) · brx2 · `$WEAP` selection and what shortens the rebind delay, on the frozen emulator (F668)

brx1's request, on the same frozen build, every result read from a shot's `$ALCD`.
- **Selection:** the gun keeps a selected slot apart from the trigger binding. A shot, a `$WEAP` and ALT set it. A
  bind that differs from the binding at the last shot switches the trigger at the next pull; otherwise the trigger
  fires the selected slot. So, bound to the USP-S after it fired, a lone `$WEAP,0` made the next pull fire the AR.
  Re-sent binds (`$BMAP,0,1`; 98 then 1; 0 then 1, with or without 98, 1 s apart) and `$AMMO,1` did not undo it; the
  USP-S's own `$WEAP` did, and a head after `$CLEAR` did. Before the bound slot fired, a `$WEAP,0` changed nothing.
  A lone `$WEAP,4` made the trigger swing the melee slot. This is the mechanism behind brx5's heavy-bind guard (SR22).
- **The rebind delay** is the release of the last pull that fired plus the SELECTED slot's tok15. A `$WEAP` of the
  target inside the swap selects it, so AR to USP-S then fires from about 530 ms (release + 500), not 880, wherever
  the write falls. Nothing else tried shortens it: `$AMMO`, `$TMP` (t4 or t8), `$QUERY`, a 98/0/98 rebind, or a
  re-arm without `$CLEAR`. A `$SPAWN` in the swap left the trigger silent through 900 ms (not bracketed).
- **A gun-owned ALT too:** after an AR tap released at 30 ms and ALT at 100, the USP-S refused at 800 and fired at
  900, although ALT + 500 had passed. So the first round after any swap waits for the later of the swap's own edge
  and the end of live fire plus the selected slot's tok15. Live fire ends at the release, or at the ALT when the ALT
  cuts a held burst (the earlier entry's "does not stack on an ALT swap" holds only for that held case).
- **For players (brx1 takes it to Tony):** after firing the AR, the phone-owned swap draws the USP-S 850 ms after the
  AR release, not in 500 ms, unless the phone re-sends the USP-S's `$WEAP` in the swap, which also refills that slot
  to its `$WEAP` magazine (the gun answers it), so the phone would need an `$AMMO` to restore the count.
- **Held-out replays on the frozen build:** fire/no-fire agrees on 562 of 584 pulls (was 559), the slot also on 550
  (was 557), levers 44 of 46 (was 46). The loss is one ALT 3 ms past a 500 ms lockout in sr1c, which flips the ALT
  parity for the rest of that step; b2's fast taps now match (the t14 floor). No disagreement points at the emulator.
- **The model** encodes the selection rule, the shorter delay and the ALT case (five tests, each red first).
