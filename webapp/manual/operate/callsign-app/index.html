The Callsign App

Battle Company's official phone app: what it does, how you build a game, and the three things nobody tells you.

benchcommunityLast verified 2026-08-27How we knowMarkdown

What the app is. Callsign turns a phone into the game host. It keeps the clock, the score and the respawn timer. It also pushes weapons and settings to the gun over Bluetooth. The gun enforces none of the rules. Take the phone out of Bluetooth range and nobody respawns, and the round never ends.

Source: protocol/brx-protocol.md §7g, §7n

  • ~1 m: keep the phone this close to its gun for the whole match. It is the game engine. bench
  • ~1 min: the typical wait for a hosted game to show up as joinable on a second phone. It round-trips through the cloud. bench
  • 69 / 100: the internal volume the app sets on the gun when it connects. bench
  • Android ≤ 10: owners report the app only works on older Android. iOS is fine. community

Source: protocol/brx-protocol.md §7g, §7b, docs/experiment-log.md, docs/reference/community-notes.md

Building and starting a game

  1. Get the top-right connection icon green ("connected"). You cannot create a game until it is, and it will not go green without a paired headset. bench
  2. Tap SELECT A GAME and swipe the category carousel: Team Arena · Battle Royale · Battle Lines · Faction Wars · Infection. bench
  3. Tap SELECT GAME MODE. Under Team Arena, for example: Arena · Team Arena · Team Snipers · Capture the Flag. bench
  4. Fill in GAME SETTINGS: primary and secondary weapon, then the rules in the table below. Press CREATE. Some modes add a step for teams, starting health, or the players-remaining display. bench
  5. Wait in the lobby while other players join from their phones, pick weapons, and hit ready. On a single device, use Start Offline Game. bench
  6. Let the host launch. Every gun goes live together, and the in-game HUD shows health, shield, ammo and weapon. bench

Source: docs/reference/callsign-ui.md, protocol/brx-protocol.md §7g, §7m, docs/experiment-log.md (2026-08-25 cap10/cap11)

Field objectives in Callsign are printed QR codes. Respawn points, weapon pickups, control points and supply drops are all paper you scan or fire at, not boxes.

Source: protocol/callsign-extract/apk-harvest.md, docs/reference/brx-extended-user-guide.md

Volume. The app's whole global settings screen is one Sound slider. On connect it sets the gun to about 69 on its internal 0–100 scale. That is loud enough for weapon audio indoors and out. Anything much below 50 makes weapon sounds effectively silent, and we measured 30 as inaudible over room noise.

Source: docs/reference/callsign-ui.md, docs/experiment-log.md (finding 6), protocol/brx-protocol.md §7b

Gotcha 1: the app renames your gun. Every session Callsign writes the name "Tactix2" to the gun. Gave a gun a custom Bluetooth name with Open BRX tools? Opening Callsign on it silently resets that name.

Source: docs/gotchas.md, protocol/brx-protocol.md §7b

Gotcha 2: "the app is flaky" is almost always the headset. With no headset paired, the app connects to the gun and drops it about a second later. There is no message, and the icon simply never turns green. Nothing is intermittent. It works exactly when the headset happens to be linked.

Source: protocol/brx-protocol.md §7m

Gotcha 3: the firmware warning is soft. Newer guns show "firmware v4.32 … supported until v2.01e". The gun is ahead of the app's list, not behind it, and games still run. Do not downgrade firmware to satisfy that message.

Source: docs/experiment-log.md (finding 3), protocol/brx-protocol.md §7b

The app shows player numbers as 1–64. The gun stores them 0–63. This only matters if you compare app numbers with developer tools.

Source: docs/experiment-log.md (2026-08-25 P2)
